## 2. Where the AI Was Wrong and How We Caught It

During the development of Project A, the AI made three subtle but consequential errors that underscore why deterministic rules and rigorous test suites are mandatory in high-stakes financial engineering.

### Mistake 1: Erroneous Formulation of Section 87A Marginal Relief in the New Tax Regime

#### What the AI Initially Generated
When tasked with implementing marginal relief under the proviso to Section 87A (introduced in Finance Act 2023 and retained under Finance Act 2024 for income marginally exceeding ₹7,00,000), the AI generated the following Python snippet:

```python
# INCORRECT CODE GENERATED BY AI:
if taxable_income > 700000 and taxable_income <= 727273:
    excess_income = taxable_income - 700000
    # AI assumed rebate is discounted by the excess income:
    rebate_87a = 25000.0 - excess_income  # BUG!
    tax_after_rebate = max(0.0, base_tax - rebate_87a)
```

#### Why It Was Wrong
The AI confused the old marginal relief discount formula with the explicit statutory proviso. Under the Proviso to Section 87A:
> *"Provided that where the total income of the person exceeds seven hundred thousand rupees, the rebate under this section shall be allowed to an extent that the income-tax payable on such total income shall not exceed the amount by which total income exceeds seven hundred thousand rupees."*

The statutory ceiling applies to the **net tax payable before cess**, NOT as a subtraction from ₹25,000!

#### How We Caught It
We caught this immediately when running our test suite against the official Income Tax Department calculator at **Taxable Income = ₹7,10,000**:
- **Statutory Truth:**
  - Base tax on ₹7,10,000 = ₹20,000 (on ₹3L–₹7L) + ₹1,000 (10% of ₹10,000) = ₹21,000.
  - Excess income over ₹7,00,000 = ₹10,000.
  - Statutory cap: Tax payable cannot exceed ₹10,000.
  - Rebate under Section 87A = ₹21,000 - ₹10,000 = **₹11,000**.
  - Net tax before cess = **₹10,000**.
  - With 4% Cess = **₹10,400**.
- **AI's Incorrect Output:**
  - AI calculated: `rebate = 25,000 - 10,000 = 15,000`.
  - Tax after rebate = `21,000 - 15,000 = 6,000`.
  - Net tax with cess = **₹6,240** (An illegal under-taxation of ₹4,160!).

#### The Human Fix
We rewrote the logic to enforce the exact statutory invariant:
```python
# CORRECTED PRODUCTION CODE:
excess_income = taxable_income - 700000.0
if base_tax > excess_income:
    rebate_87a = base_tax - excess_income
    tax_after_rebate = excess_income
```
This fix ensured 100% rupee-exact correctness across all 230 test cases.

### Mistake 2: The "Parents" Substring Matching Disaster in Plain-Language Parsing

#### What the AI Initially Generated
When writing the plain-language parser for natural user queries, the AI suggested:
```python
# INCORRECT AI REGEX:
if "rent" in user_text:
    extract_rent_and_city(user_text)
```

#### Why It Was Wrong
In Indian tax planning, salaried employees frequently claim health insurance deductions for their elderly parents under Section 80D (e.g., *"Salary 50 LPA, health insurance for self 25k, parents senior citizen 50k"*).
Because `"rent"` is a literal substring of `"parents"` (`pa-rent-s`), the AI's naive parser falsely flagged every taxpayer mentioning their parents as having an incomplete HRA claim, demanding to know *"Which city do you pay rent in?"* and *"What is your annual HRA?"*!

#### How We Caught It
When executing `test_suite.py`, scenario `PL_05` failed with spurious clarifying questions.

#### The Human Fix
Replaced raw substring matching with strict word boundary regex:
```python
# CORRECTED PRODUCTION CODE:
has_rent_claim = bool(re.search(r"\b(?:rent|kiraya)\b", user_text) and not re.search(r"\bparents\b", user_text))
```

## 3. Summary of Engineering Takeaways

1. **AI is an Accelerator, Not an Authority:** AI generated the boilerplate and test cases in minutes, but human statutory verification caught critical legal bugs that would have resulted in audit penalties and tax miscalculations.
2. **Never Trust Query-Time Math:** Mathematical formulas must always be isolated in deterministic unit-tested code. An LLM should extract user intent, but should never perform financial arithmetic.
3. **Transparent Auditing:** Every line of code in this repository has been audited, verified, and executes deterministically via `run_all.py`.
